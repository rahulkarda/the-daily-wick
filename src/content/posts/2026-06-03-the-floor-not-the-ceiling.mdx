## The Idea

The floor is not your goal. It is not your ideal output, your best day, or the version of yourself you're working toward. The floor is simply the minimum you do on every day, including the bad ones.

Two pages. Ten minutes of practice. One rep. A single paragraph. The floor is defined not by what it produces but by what it protects — the unbroken thread of the practice itself.

This distinction matters because most people set targets, not floors. Targets are useful for direction, but they collapse under the weight of a hard week. When you miss three days against a target, the target starts to feel like evidence of failure. When you miss three days and then return to the floor, you haven't failed — you've just come back to the minimum. The practice continues.

Jerry Seinfeld's "don't break the chain" principle works not because of the chain, but because of the floor underneath it. The chain is the goal. The floor is what keeps you able to build another chain after you've broken one.

Great days are not guaranteed. The floor is. And over months and years, the floor is what compounds.

## One Question

What is the single smallest action that would count as "showing up" for the practice that matters most to you right now?

## Today's Action

1. Name the practice — writing, exercise, learning, whatever it is.
2. Set the floor explicitly: the minimum that counts as doing it. Make it so small it feels almost embarrassing.
3. For the next seven days, hold only the floor. Not the ideal. The floor. See what that feels like at the end of the week.

## Go Deeper

The ceiling is where ambition lives. The floor is where the practice survives. You cannot raise the ceiling without first building the floor.
