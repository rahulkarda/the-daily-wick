## The Idea

The first glance classifies. The second glance sees.

Most reading is first-glance reading: we recognize the category of thing — email, complaint, update, request — and respond to the category rather than the specific thing in front of us. We've processed the type before we've read the instance. This is fast and often adequate. It's also how misunderstandings compound, how people feel unheard, and how obvious errors get walked past.

There's a different quality of attention that doesn't rush to classify. It notices what doesn't fit the category — the detail that changes the interpretation, the sentence that modifies everything that came before it, the exception that reveals something real. Simone Weil called this kind of attention a form of love: you give the thing your full presence without immediately assimilating it into what you already know.

It requires slowing down in a specific way. Not reading slower, necessarily — but suspending the urge to have already understood. Staying with the text or the face or the situation without immediately reaching for the nearest familiar frame.

The cost of the first glance isn't ignorance. It's the confidence that you've already seen what's there.

## One Question

Where in your life are you responding to a category of thing rather than the actual thing in front of you?

## Today's Action

Choose one thing you read today — an email, a message, a short article — and read it twice. On the second pass, look specifically for what you skipped or assumed on the first. It will be there.

## Go Deeper

The second glance is not about being slower. It's about being honest that the first glance was a guess, and that some things are worth the cost of a second one.
