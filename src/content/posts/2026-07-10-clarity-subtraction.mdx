## The Idea

We often chase clarity by adding: more information, more frameworks, more goals. But true clarity often emerges through subtraction. It's the disciplined act of removing the non-essential, the distracting, the merely good, to make space for the truly great. Think of a sculptor chipping away stone to reveal the form within, not adding clay to create it. This isn't about minimalism for its own sake, but about identifying and eliminating the noise that obscures what genuinely matters to you.

## One Question

What is one thing you could deliberately remove from your current commitments, information streams, or habits to gain a sharper focus on what remains?

## Today's Action

1. Review your last week's calendar: Identify one recurring meeting or obligation that adds more complexity than value.
2. Open your phone: Delete one app that consistently pulls your attention without offering significant return.
3. Clear your desk or digital workspace: Remove three items (physical or digital files) you haven't touched in a month and don't foresee needing soon.

## Go Deeper

The things we remove often reveal the shape of our true priorities more profoundly than the things we accumulate. Clarity isn't always found by seeking new light, but by eliminating the shadows.
