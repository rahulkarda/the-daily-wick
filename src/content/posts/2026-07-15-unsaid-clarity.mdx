## The Idea

We often assume clarity means saying everything, explaining every detail, leaving no room for ambiguity. But true clarity can sometimes emerge from what is *unsaid*. Think of a sculptor removing material to reveal the form within, or a composer using silence to emphasize a note. The unsaid forces attention onto what remains, cutting through the noise of excessive explanation.

Consider a team meeting where endless slides and data obscure the core decision. A leader who can distill the situation to one crucial question, leaving the extraneous details implied or for later, provides a different, more powerful kind of clarity. It's not about being vague; it's about disciplined omission.

## One Question

What are you over-explaining or over-complicating that could be made clearer by saying less?

## Today's Action

1. Review an upcoming email or conversation where you intend to convey an important point.
2. Identify one sentence or paragraph you can remove without losing the core message.
3. Send/speak the pared-down version. Notice if the essential point lands more sharply.

## Go Deeper

Clarity isn't just about presence; it's about absence. The discipline of the unsaid forces us to confront what is truly indispensable, making the signal undeniable.
