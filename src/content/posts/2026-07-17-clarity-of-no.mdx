## The Idea
We often pursue clarity by adding: more information, more goals, more strategies. But true clarity, the kind that cuts through noise, is almost always achieved through subtraction. It’s not about finding the perfect 'yes,' but mastering the deliberate 'no.' Every commitment you make, every project you take on, every obligation you accept, comes at a hidden cost: the opportunities you *cannot* pursue, the focus you *cannot* maintain on what truly matters. This isn't just about time; it's about cognitive bandwidth and emotional energy. The discipline of 'no' is the active rejection of good things to make space for the great things. It’s the constant pruning that allows the essential branches to flourish.

## One Question
What is one 'good' thing you are currently doing that is preventing you from doing one 'great' thing?

## Today's Action
1. Review your calendar for the next week. Identify one meeting or commitment that, while perhaps useful, isn't essential to your core priorities.
2. Decline it, delegate it, or reschedule it for a later date. Be polite but firm.
3. Use the reclaimed time (even if just 15 minutes) to work on your highest-leverage task, or simply to sit in quiet reflection.

## Go Deeper
Clarity isn't found in expansion, but in careful contraction. The shape of what you truly value becomes visible in the negative space of all the things you choose to let go.
