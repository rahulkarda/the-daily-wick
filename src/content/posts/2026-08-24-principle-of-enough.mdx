## The Idea

We often equate resilience with an endless capacity to endure, to push through, to always find more. But true resilience isn't about an infinite well of grit; it's about the wisdom to recognize a boundary. It's the skill of knowing when 'enough' has been reached – enough effort, enough stress, enough exposure to a problem. This isn't quitting; it's a strategic withdrawal, a deliberate preservation of resources. The person who burns out isn't lacking resilience; they're lacking the discernment to say, "This is enough for now, I need to regroup." This recognition allows for recovery, preventing the deeper fractures that come from constant, unexamined pushing.

## One Question

Where in your current challenge are you pushing past the point of diminishing returns, mistaking exhaustion for effort?

## Today's Action

1. Identify one task or commitment you're currently overextending yourself on.
2. Define a clear, reasonable stopping point for it today – not based on perfection, but on 'enough'.
3. When you reach that point, consciously stop. Redirect the saved energy to rest or something restorative.

## Go Deeper

Resilience isn't just about bending without breaking; it's also about knowing when to step back, to mend, and to reinforce. The strongest structures aren't those that can bear infinite weight, but those designed to last through careful management of their loads.
