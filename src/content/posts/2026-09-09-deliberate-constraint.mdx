## The Idea
We often equate improvement with acquisition: more tools, more skills, more information. Yet, true craftspeople understand the power of deliberate constraint. It's the chef who limits herself to three ingredients, the writer who commits to a haiku, or the designer who works only in black and white. These aren't limitations to be overcome, but foundations to build upon.

By consciously removing options, we force ourselves to innovate within boundaries. This isn't about doing less; it's about doing *more* with *less*. It hones our sensitivity, sharpens our choices, and reveals the essence of what we're trying to achieve, preventing the dilution that comes from endless possibility.

## One Question
What is one resource, tool, or option you could intentionally remove from your current project to force a deeper kind of creativity?

## Today's Action
1. Identify a small creative task or problem you're facing today – writing an email, planning a meal, sketching an idea.
2. Impose a specific, artificial constraint on yourself (e.g., "use only five sentences," "cook with only two spices," "draw without lifting your pen").
3. Execute the task under this constraint, noticing how your approach changes.

## Go Deeper
Constraint isn't the opposite of freedom; it's often the prerequisite for a more profound and focused kind of freedom. When we limit our external choices, we expand our internal ingenuity.
