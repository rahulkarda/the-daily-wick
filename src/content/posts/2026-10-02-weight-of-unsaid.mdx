## The Idea

We often perceive direct, honest feedback as a form of friction – something that will cause discomfort, resistance, or even damage to a relationship. So, we hold back, soften the blow, or avoid the conversation entirely. The irony is that this *avoidance* of initial friction often creates a far greater, more insidious resistance over time. Unsaid truths don't disappear; they accumulate, forming a dense, unacknowledged weight that silently strains connections and hinders progress. This "unsaid friction" makes future interactions more difficult, builds resentment, and ensures that the very issue we tried to avoid addressing only grows larger.

## One Question

What truth are you holding back, believing you're preventing friction, but are actually creating a heavier burden for yourself and others?

## Today's Action

1. Identify one small, low-stakes piece of feedback you've been hesitant to share.
2. Rehearse a single, direct sentence that expresses it, focusing on observable behavior and its impact, not judgment.
3. Deliver it gently, but clearly, to the relevant person today. Notice the immediate, lighter feeling.

## Go Deeper
The real cost of avoiding direct friction is the slow erosion of trust and the quiet accumulation of unspoken resentments. Sometimes, the most freeing act is to introduce a small, intentional resistance.
