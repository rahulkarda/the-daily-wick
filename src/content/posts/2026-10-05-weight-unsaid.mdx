## The Idea

We often avoid difficult conversations, sidestepping the uncomfortable friction of directness. It feels easier in the moment to leave things unsaid, to offer a vague reply, or to skirt around a truth. But this avoidance doesn't eliminate friction; it merely displaces it. The unsaid word, the unresolved tension, the implicit expectation – these don't vanish. They accumulate, like silt in a riverbed, subtly changing the flow of relationships and projects. This hidden friction is far more corrosive than the brief, sharp clarity of an honest exchange. It erodes trust, fosters misunderstanding, and burdens us with unspoken anxieties, making future interactions even harder. The momentary ease of avoidance always comes with a compounding interest of future difficulty.

## One Question

What truth are you postponing today, thinking you are avoiding friction when you are actually storing it?

## Today's Action

1. Identify one small, specific thing you've been hesitant to say to a colleague, friend, or family member.
2. Draft it clearly and concisely, focusing on observation, not accusation.
3. Deliver it directly and kindly. Notice the immediate, albeit brief, discomfort, and the subsequent clarity.

## Go Deeper

The most important things are often the hardest to articulate. The weight of what's unsaid doesn't disappear; it settles within us, affecting our presence and our peace. Choose the small friction of truth over the heavy burden of silence.
