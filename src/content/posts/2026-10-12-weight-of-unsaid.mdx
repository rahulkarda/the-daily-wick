## The Idea

We often perceive difficult conversations as a form of friction to be avoided. The awkwardness, the potential for disagreement, the discomfort—these are signals to detour, to postpone, or to simply remain silent. Yet, what we avoid in the short term often accumulates into a much heavier, more insidious resistance. The unsaid words don't disappear; they solidify into unspoken resentments, unaddressed assumptions, and growing distances. Think of a small stone in your shoe that, ignored, becomes a debilitating blister. The initial, minor friction of addressing it upfront is a fraction of the cost of enduring it until it becomes a real wound. Necessary friction today prevents debilitating resistance tomorrow.

## One Question

What truth, left unspoken, is already creating more distance than the difficult conversation itself ever could?

## Today's Action

1. Identify one low-stakes conversation you've been avoiding with a colleague or friend.
2. Draft a single, clear sentence articulating the core point you need to make.
3. Approach the person today and deliver that sentence, then listen without interruption.

## Go Deeper

The illusion of peace is a fragile thing, often shattered by the very silence that seemed to preserve it. True peace, in relationships, is built on the sturdy foundation of honest, if sometimes uncomfortable, exchange.
