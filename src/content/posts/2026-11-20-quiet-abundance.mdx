## The Idea

We often frame gratitude as a reaction to extraordinary events: the big promotion, the new house, the grand gesture. But true, sustained gratitude rarely thrives on such peaks. Instead, it flourishes in the quiet abundance of the ordinary. It’s the unnoticed hum of the refrigerator, the predictable warmth of the morning coffee, the steady presence of a familiar face. These are not exciting, but they are *enough*. They are the reliable foundations that make bigger moments possible, and their consistent presence is a wealth we rarely account for. To notice them is to recognize that we are often far richer than we allow ourselves to feel.

## One Question

What steady, unremarkable comfort in your life do you consistently take for granted?

## Today's Action

1. For the next 24 hours, identify three small, everyday things you use or encounter without conscious thought (e.g., a warm blanket, reliable internet, a sturdy chair).
2. As you use each, pause for five seconds. Acknowledge its function and its consistent presence.
3. Briefly consider what it would be like if that item or service were suddenly absent.

## Go Deeper

Gratitude isn't just about what's *added* to your life; it's profoundly about what's *already there* and sustained. The shift from seeking novel blessings to appreciating enduring ones changes everything.
