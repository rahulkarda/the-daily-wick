## The Idea

We often feel gratitude deeply. A friend offers help without asking, a colleague steps in, a partner makes a quiet sacrifice. We register it, we appreciate it internally, and then... we move on. But unexpressed gratitude is like a gift left unwrapped; its potential remains dormant. The weight of that unacknowledged kindness can linger, not as appreciation, but as a subtle burden. It's the feeling that you *should* have said something, that you *owe* something, which can be heavier than the simple act of saying "thank you."

## One Question

What unacknowledged act of kindness or support is still lingering in your mind, waiting for its full expression?

## Today's Action

1. Identify one person who has recently done something for you that you deeply appreciated but didn't explicitly acknowledge.
2. Take two minutes to send them a short, specific message (text, email, or in-person) expressing your thanks. Be clear about *what* they did and *how* it impacted you.
3. Notice the subtle shift in your own feeling after you send it.

## Go Deeper

Gratitude, fully realized, is a circuit. It begins with reception, but it completes only with expression. Until then, it's not truly yours, or theirs. It's just a feeling waiting to become a connection.
